# Water

> **Assume every untreated source is contaminated.** Clear water is not safe water. Chemical disinfection does not remove heavy metals, fuel, pesticides or radiological particles.

## Planning figures
| Need | Litres/person/day |
|---|---|
| Drinking only, temperate | 2–3 |
| Drinking + basic hygiene | 4 |
| Hot climate / heavy work | 6–8 |
| Children, pregnancy, illness | add 1–2 |
| Pets | 0.5–2 depending on size |

Store **14 days** where possible: 56 L/person at 4 L/day. Rotate stored water every 6 months. Use food-grade containers, keep out of sunlight, and label the fill date.

## Sources, in order of preference
1. Sealed commercial bottled water.
2. Stored municipal water (if the system is not compromised).
3. Rainwater collected on clean surfaces.
4. Springs and fast-flowing streams upstream of settlements.
5. Lakes and slow water.
6. Groundwater (well, seep).
7. Floodwater, stagnant water, sea water — **last resort / not drinkable without desalination**.

Never drink from a source downstream of a sewer outfall, industrial site, mine, or recent flood. If it smells like fuel or chemicals, do not treat and drink it; find another source.

## Treatment
### Boil
Rolling boil for **1 minute** below 2,000 m (6,500 ft); **3 minutes** above. Cool covered. Boiling kills pathogens but concentrates chemicals and does not remove them.

### Filter
Use a certified absolute filter (≤0.2 µm for bacteria/protozoa; virus removal requires additional treatment). Pre-filter cloudy water through cloth. Filters clog; carry a backflush syringe and spare cartridge. **Filters do not remove dissolved chemicals.**

### Chlorine
Use plain unscented household bleach (5.25–8.25% sodium hypochlorite):
- **5.25–6%:** 2 drops/L clear; 4 drops/L cloudy.
- **8.25%:** 1 drop/L clear; 2 drops/L cloudy.
Stir, wait **30 minutes** (60 if cloudy or cold). A faint chlorine smell should remain. If not, repeat once. Do not use scented, splashless, colour-safe or additive bleach. For larger volumes, use the console's bleach calculator and a chlorine test kit if available.

### SODIS
Clear PET bottles filled with filtered water, laid in full sun for **6 hours** (2 days if cloudy). Only works on clear water, in strong sunlight, with bottles no larger than 2 L. Less reliable than boiling/filtration. Do not use on chemically contaminated water.

### Desalination / distillation
Sea water and urine require distillation or reverse osmosis. A simple solar still produces small amounts; do not rely on it for a group. Never drink sea water.

## Storage and distribution
- Use dedicated containers; never store fuel or chemicals in water bottles.
- Keep a tap or dispenser to avoid dipping cups into stores.
- Disinfect containers with a weak bleach solution, rinse with safe water, and dry.
- Freeze water bottles to extend fridge/freezer life during outages.
- In freezing weather, store water where it will not burst containers; leave headspace.

## Finding water
- Follow terrain: water flows downhill; look for green vegetation, animal trails, and confluences.
- Morning dew can be collected with cloth.
- Solar stills and transpiration bags yield small amounts; they are morale tools, not supply.
- Snow/ice: melt before drinking to avoid hypothermia; do not eat snow.
- In coastal areas, look for freshwater seeps and springs above the high-tide line; test carefully.
- Never assume a desert seep is safe.

## Emergency oral rehydration
For diarrhoea/vomiting, use ORS rather than plain water. Per litre of safe water: **2.6 g salt + 13.5 g glucose + 1.5 g KCl** (WHO low-osmolarity). Household approximation: **6 level tsp table sugar + ½ level tsp salt per litre** (6 tsp sugar ≈ 25 g sucrose, matching the 13.5 g glucose target). Stir well, sip frequently, and do not add extra salt. Seek care for blood in stool, persistent vomiting, lethargy, or any dehydrated infant.
