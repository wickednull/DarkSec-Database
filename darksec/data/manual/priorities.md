# Survival Priorities

> **The rule:** stop, think, plan, act. Panic burns energy and makes bad decisions. Most survival situations are resolved by staying found, staying warm, staying hydrated and not making the situation worse.

## 1. Immediate threats
- Move away from the hazard: fire, smoke, traffic, unstable structure, rising water, live electrical lines, hostile persons.
- Control severe bleeding first. Airway and breathing come next. A patient who is bleeding out does not benefit from a splint.
- Prevent further injury. Do not become a second casualty.

## 2. Stay found
If you are lost but not in immediate danger, **stop**. Mark your location. Make yourself visible and audible. Searchers can find a stationary person far faster than they can catch a moving one. Use three of anything: three whistle blasts, three fires, three flashes, three marks.

Tell someone your plan before you leave. Leave a note with route, destination and return time. A written check-in schedule with a trusted person is worth more than most gear.

## 3. Core temperature
Hypothermia and heat illness kill quickly. Shelter from wind and rain, insulate from the ground, change out of wet clothing, and warm the trunk. In heat, move to shade, wet the skin, fan, and sip water. **Do not** use alcohol to warm a cold person.

## 4. Water
A person can survive weeks without food but only days without water. Plan 4 L per person per day as a minimum (drinking plus basic hygiene); more in heat, at altitude, during illness or pregnancy. Treat all unknown water. Boiling and certified filtration are preferred; chemical disinfection is a backup.

## 5. Food
Food is rarely the first emergency. Ration steadily rather than starving and bingeing. Do not eat unknown plants, mushrooms or shellfish. Wild food is a supplement, not a plan. Protect stored food from animals and contamination.

## 6. Signal and communicate
Conserve battery. Use the highest-probability method first: phone text, emergency beacon, radio, signal mirror, whistle, ground signals. Give **location, situation, number of people, injuries, and what you need**. Write it down before transmitting.

## 7. Sanitation
The second disaster is often disease. Keep human waste away from water and shelter. Wash hands. Disinfect wounds. Store waste securely. A bucket, bags and cover material are critical infrastructure.

## 8. Security
Avoid, detect, de-escalate, escape. Violence is a legal and moral last resort. Keep a low profile, protect documents and supplies, and coordinate with trusted neighbours. Do not create unnecessary adversaries.

## 9. Decision discipline
- Set a decision deadline.
- Use a simple written plan: who, what, when, where, how, and the fallback.
- Rotate rest and watch. Fatigue destroys judgement.
- Reassess when the facts change, not when you feel like it.

## The five-minute kit check
Water, fire, shelter, light, first aid, knife, navigation, communication. If you have those, you can solve most field problems long enough to get help.
