# Knots & Rigging

> Practise with gloves and in the dark. A knot you cannot tie under stress is not in your kit. Inspect ropes for cuts, glazing, UV damage and abrasion; retire load-bearing rope after shock loads or chemical exposure.

## 1. Bowline — fixed loop
**Use:** rescue loop, tarp anchor, hauling.
1. Make a small loop in the standing part.
2. Pass the working end up through the loop, around the standing part, and back down.
3. Tighten. The working end should end up inside the loop. Add a stopper knot for critical loads.

## 2. Clove hitch — attach to a post
**Use:** starting/ending lashings, tarp poles, quick attachment.
1. Wrap the rope around the post, crossing over itself.
2. Wrap again above the first turn, tuck the end under the second turn.
3. Tighten both ends. It can slip under variable load; add a stopper or two half-hitches.

## 3. Taut-line hitch — adjustable tension
**Use:** tent guylines, tarp tension, adjustable ridgeline.
1. Attach to the anchor with a round turn.
2. Wrap the working end twice inside the loop toward the anchor, then once outside.
3. Tighten and slide to adjust. Load it to set.

## 4. Figure-eight — stopper
**Use:** prevent rope from running through a device or eye; rescue systems.
1. Make a loop, pass the end around the standing part, then through the loop.
2. Tighten. For a figure-eight follow-through, retrace the path with the other end.

## 5. Square (reef) knot — bind a package
**Use:** bandages, bundles, non-critical binding.
1. Right over left, then left over right. The two ends should exit on the same side.
2. **Not for load-bearing or joining ropes.** It can capsize and spill.

## 6. Sheet bend — join unequal ropes
**Use:** join two ropes of different diameter; net making.
1. Make a bight in the thicker rope.
2. Pass the thinner rope up through the bight, around both legs, and under itself.
3. Tighten. Add a double sheet bend for security.

## 7. Prusik — friction hitch
**Use:** ascent/descent backup, tensioning, rescue.
1. Tie a loop of cord with a double fisherman's knot.
2. Wrap the loop around the main rope 3 times (4–6 for slippery rope), passing the loop through itself.
3. Dress and load. It grips one way and slides the other. **Never rely on a single friction hitch for life safety without training.**

## 8. Trucker's hitch — mechanical advantage
**Use:** lashing loads, tight tarps, ridgelines.
1. Tie a loop (or use a pulley) in the standing part.
2. Pass the working end around the anchor and back through the loop.
3. Pull tight, then secure with two half-hitches. Gives roughly 2:1–3:1 advantage.

## Lashings
- **Square lashing:** bind perpendicular poles.
- **Diagonal lashing:** cross braces; start with a timber hitch.
- **Shear lashing:** parallel poles that open into an A-frame.
- Tighten progressively; finish with a clove hitch and frapping turns.

## Rigging safety
- **Working load limit (WLL)** is far below breaking strength: use a safety factor of 5–10 for human loads.
- Avoid sharp edges; use edge protection. Do not stand under a suspended load.
- Anchor to living trees carefully (use wide straps, not cable) and never to dead limbs.
- Check knots after loading. Retie if slipped or damaged.
- Pulleys reduce friction and increase mechanical advantage; a carabiner alone adds friction.
- For rescue, use certified equipment and trained systems. Improvised ropes are not life-safety equipment.
