# CBRN

> **Get inside, stay inside, stay tuned.** For a radiological or nuclear release, sheltering is usually more protective than fleeing through the plume. Follow official instructions. This manual is a field reference, not a substitute for trained CBRN personnel.

## Recognition
- **Chemical:** unexplained odour, coloured vapour, dead insects/birds, droplets on surfaces, mass casualties with pinpoint pupils/salivation/breathing difficulty.
- **Biological:** unusual clusters of illness, fever, respiratory or gastrointestinal symptoms, public-health alerts.
- **Radiological/nuclear:** flash, blast, mushroom cloud, dust, unexplained radiation alarms, heat/electrical failure.
- **Explosive/improvised:** do not approach, touch, or use a radio near a suspected device. Evacuate and call professionals.

## Immediate actions
1. **Move crosswind and uphill** away from the release if outside. Avoid low ground and confined spaces.
2. **Get inside** the nearest intact building. Put as much mass and distance between you and the outside as possible.
3. **Seal:** close windows, doors, vents, fireplace dampers; turn off HVAC; tape/plastic over gaps if available.
4. **Shelter** in an interior room, ideally a basement or the core of a multi-storey building. Avoid windows.
5. **Monitor** battery radio, NOAA, phone alerts. Do not go outside until told.
6. **Decontaminate** if exposed: remove outer clothing, bag it, shower with lukewarm water and soap, flush eyes.
7. **Do not eat or drink** exposed food/water. Use sealed supplies.

## Radiation basics
- **Dose** is energy absorbed per unit mass. 1 Gy = 1 Sv for gamma/beta; 1 Sv = 100 rem; 1 mSv = 100 mrem.
- **Dose rate** is µSv/h or mrem/h. Dose = rate × time.
- **Protection:** time (minimise), distance (maximise; dose falls roughly with the square of distance), shielding (mass).
- **Typical background:** 0.05–0.3 µSv/h (varies with altitude/geology). A chest X-ray ≈ 0.1 mSv.
- **Acute radiation syndrome:** >1 Sv whole-body (nausea, vomiting, fatigue, burns, hair loss). >4 Sv is life-threatening; >8 Sv often fatal without advanced care.
- **Instrument:** Geiger-Müller tubes measure beta/gamma; scintillators are more sensitive. Calibrate and know units. A dosimeter accumulates dose.

## Fallout
- Early fallout is intensely radioactive and decays quickly. The **7:10 rule** approximates a 10× reduction for every 7× time after detonation.
- Shelter protection factor (PF): dose inside = dose outside / PF. Basements and interior walls can provide PF 10–100+; a vehicle is poor.
- Stay sheltered for at least 24–48 h unless authorities say otherwise; the first hours are the most dangerous.
- Cover food and water; use sealed containers. Avoid dust.
- Do not evacuate through fallout unless instructed; know routes and reception centres.

## KI (potassium iodide)
KI saturates the thyroid so it absorbs less radioactive iodine. It does **not** protect against external gamma radiation or other isotopes.

**FDA age-based dose, once every 24 h while exposure continues:**
- Adults (including pregnant/lactating): **130 mg**
- Children 3–18 y: **65 mg**
- Infants 1 month–3 y: **32 mg**
- Newborns birth–1 month: **16 mg**

Use only when directed by authorities, unless the risk clearly outweighs the risk of thyroid cancer. Do not use with iodine allergy, dermatitis herpetiformis, or certain thyroid conditions without medical advice. KI is not a general radiation antidote.

## Chemical exposure
- Move upwind/uphill; remove contaminated clothing; flush skin/eyes with copious water.
- Do not induce vomiting after ingestion unless directed. Do not give anything by mouth to an unconscious person.
- Nerve agent signs: pinpoint pupils, salivation, tearing, urination, diarrhoea, vomiting, bronchospasm, convulsions. Antidotes (atropine, pralidoxime) are prescription/agency items; use only with training.
- Do not mix chemicals. Do not use bleach on ammonia/acid. Do not enter a contaminated area to rescue without PPE and training.

## Biological
- Isolation and hand hygiene are the first defences.
- N95/KN95 masks, eye protection, gloves; ventilate rooms; disinfect surfaces.
- Support hydration, fever and breathing; seek care for red flags.
- Do not use antibiotics indiscriminately. Follow public-health guidance and prescriptions.
- Safe burials and waste management matter. Keep rodents and insects away from food and shelter.

## PPE and decon
- **Level C:** splash suit, gloves, boots, eye/face protection, air-purifying respirator. **Level B:** SCBA plus chemical-resistant suit. **Level A:** fully encapsulated SCBA. Do not improvise beyond your training.
- Decon corridor: remove outer clothing → dry decon → wet decon with soap/water → rinse → dry → monitor.
- Bag all waste; label it. Do not contaminate the clean area.
- Heat stress inside PPE is severe; rotate teams, hydrate, monitor.

## Shelter checklist
- Water and food for 14 days, sealed.
- Battery/hand-crank radio, spare batteries.
- KI if available and appropriate, first aid, medications.
- Plastic sheeting, tape, scissors, towels, buckets, bags.
- Flashlight, dust masks, gloves, sturdy shoes.
- CO alarm, fire extinguisher, sanitation.
- Written contact plan and meeting point.
