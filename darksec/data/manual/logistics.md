# Logistics & Sustainment

> **Amateurs talk tactics; professionals talk logistics.** Food, water, fuel, batteries, medicine and information all have a shelf life and a consumption rate. Count, rotate, and plan for loss.

## Inventory discipline
- Track **item, quantity, unit, minimum, expiry, location, owner**.
- Two thresholds: **minimum** (reorder/ration trigger) and **target** (full stock).
- Rotate stock: first in, first out. Mark dates. Inspect every 6 months.
- Keep a paper copy of the inventory outside the device. A dead battery must not erase your plan.
- Separate **home stock**, **go bags**, **vehicle kit**, **caches** and **team equipment**. Do not double-count.

## Consumption planning
- Water: 4 L/person/day (drinking + basic hygiene).
- Food: 2,000 kcal/person/day baseline; 2,500–3,500 for cold/heavy work.
- Fuel: record generator hours and fuel burn; plan for 25% reserve.
- Batteries: count by size and device; rechargeables need a charger and power.
- Medical: 30 days of critical meds if possible; rotate.
- Sanitation: bags, lime, soap, menstrual products, diapers.
- Comms: spare batteries, antenna parts, cables, connectors, radio manual.

## Caches
- Choose locations that are legal to access, stable, discreet and not prone to flood/fire/theft.
- Protect from moisture (sealed container, desiccant), pests (metal/plastic), heat and freezing.
- Record coordinates (MGRS + lat/lon), contents, date, and access notes in the encrypted vault.
- Split critical supplies across multiple caches; do not put everything in one place.
- Inspect annually; rotate food/water/batteries.
- Tell only the people who must know. A cache is a liability if compromised.

## Vehicle logistics
- Keep the tank above half; store fuel safely and legally.
- Maintenance: tyres, fluids, belts, filters, battery, lights, brakes.
- Load plan: heavy items low and forward; secure everything; keep exits clear.
- Tools: jack, spare, plug kit, compressor, jumper pack, tow strap, shovel.
- Documents: registration, insurance, maps, contact list.
- Never carry fuel inside the passenger compartment.

## Team logistics
- Assign roles: leader, medic, comms, logistics, security, records.
- Establish a watch schedule and rest plan. Fatigue is a logistics failure.
- Ration by need, not by rank. Children, pregnant/lactating women, sick and injured need more water and care.
- Plan for pets: food, water, leash, carrier, vaccination records.
- Plan for evacuation: who carries what, rally points, check-in times, alternate routes.

## Field trip accountability
- Record the route, checkpoints, party size, planned departure and conservative return time.
- Give a trusted off-site contact the printed plan, check-in schedule, and an agreed overdue action before departure.
- Start the trip in the console; make a local check-in at each planned interval; finish it when the party is safely back.
- A missed check-in or return deadline is only a warning on this device. The console does not track the party, transmit a check-in, or alert the contact. Keep a paper plan and use an external communication method.

## Barter and trade
- Useful trade goods: salt, sugar, coffee, lighters, batteries, candles, soap, sewing kits, cordage, fishing line, antibiotics if legally prescribed, tools.
- Trade fairly and safely; meet in public, bring a friend, do not display large stockpiles.
- Money may be useless or essential; keep small bills and coins.
- Do not trade critical medical supplies or weapons illegally.

## Records and after-action
- Log every significant event: weather, supply use, incidents, decisions, radio traffic.
- A daily SITREP prevents drift: situation, supplies, personnel, actions, needs.
- Review weekly: what broke, what ran out, what was wasted, what to change.
- Photograph documents and cache contents; store encrypted.

## The console
**Logistics** tracks inventory, expiry and low stock; **Map** stores caches and waypoints; **Log** records events; **SITREP** rolls up readiness. Export a backup before and after field exercises.
