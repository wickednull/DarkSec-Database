# Weather

> **The sky is a sensor.** Your barometer, wind, clouds and senses often beat a distant forecast, especially when the network is down.

## The weather station
Minimum: thermometer, barometer, anemometer, rain gauge, hygrometer. A sling psychrometer or wet-bulb thermometer adds humidity. Record at the same times daily, ideally 07:00, 13:00 and 19:00 local. Note cloud type, cover, wind direction/speed, precipitation and visibility.

## Pressure
- **Rising rapidly** (>3.5 hPa/3 h): improving, often windier.
- **Rising slowly:** improving.
- **Steady:** little change.
- **Falling slowly:** deterioration likely within 12–24 h.
- **Falling rapidly:** storm likely; secure camp and expect strong wind.
- Pressure falls about 1 hPa per 8.5 m of elevation; correct to sea level if comparing stations.
- High pressure in winter often means cold, clear nights; low pressure means wind and precipitation.

## Clouds
| Cloud | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cirrus / cirrostratus | High, thin; approach of a front; halo warns of rain within 12–24 h |
| Altostratus | Thickening veil; rain likely |
| Cumulus humilis | Fair weather |
| Cumulus congestus / cumulonimbus | Showers, thunderstorms, gust fronts, hail, lightning |
| Stratocumulus | Stable, grey, occasional drizzle |
| Stratus | Low grey sheet; drizzle/fog |
| Nimbostratus | Continuous rain/snow |
| Lenticular | Strong mountain winds |
| Mammatus | Severe storm aftermath; unstable air |

## Wind
- **Backing** (counter-clockwise) often precedes a frontal passage in the Northern Hemisphere; **veering** follows it.
- Gust fronts from thunderstorms can exceed 100 km/h. Get off ridges and water.
- In mountains, valley winds reverse day/night; katabatic winds drain cold air at night.
- Use the Beaufort scale if you lack an anemometer.

## Thunderstorms and lightning
- Cumulonimbus with a dark base, green tint, shelf cloud, or sudden wind shift = severe.
- **30/30 rule:** if time from flash to thunder is <30 s, take shelter. Wait 30 minutes after the last thunder before resuming exposed activity.
- Lightning can strike up to 16 km from the parent storm. Avoid ridges, lone trees, water, metal fences and open fields.
- Shelter in a building or a vehicle with the windows up. In the open, crouch on the balls of your feet, feet together, away from others.

## Heat and cold
- **Wind chill** accelerates hypothermia; **heat index** and **wet-bulb** predict heat stress.
- Wet-bulb >35 °C is lethal within hours even at rest; shade, water and cooling are critical.
- Measure **dew point**: close to the temperature means fog/rain and poor evaporative cooling.
- Inversions trap pollution and smoke in valleys.

## Fog and visibility
- Radiation fog forms on clear, calm nights, often in valleys; burns off after sunrise.
- Advection fog forms when warm moist air moves over cold ground/water.
- Fog at night + freezing temperatures = black ice.

## Forecasting without a network
1. Record pressure, wind, cloud and temperature every 3 hours.
2. Compare pressure tendency and cloud succession.
3. Watch the horizon: a dark, lowering cloud bank to the west (mid-latitudes) signals a front.
4. Use persistence and climatology: what has the weather done at this season?
5. Use nature signs cautiously: smoke behaviour, bird/insect activity, pine cones. They are not reliable on their own.
6. When online, cache NWS alerts; when offline, rely on your log and radio.

## Severe weather actions
- **Tornado:** shelter in a basement or interior room, away from windows; protect your head. Mobile home: leave and go to a sturdy building.
- **Hurricane/typhoon:** shelter from wind and surge; expect water and power loss; evacuate if told.
- **Flood:** move to high ground; never drive through water.
- **Wildfire:** evacuate early; smoke is a health hazard; N95 masks help particles, not CO.
- **Winter storm:** stay put unless you must move; insulate, vent heat sources, check CO.

## The console
**Weather** logs observations, computes wind chill/heat index/dew point/wet bulb/humidex/pressure altitude, shows pressure tendency and Beaufort, and caches NWS alerts when online. **Nav** gives sun/moon/twilight for planning. **SDR** receives NOAA weather radio and APT satellite imagery.
