# Food & Foraging

> **Wild food is not a plan.** It is a supplement. Misidentifying a plant or mushroom can kill you. The console deliberately does not claim to identify plants from photos.

## Calories and planning
- Baseline: **2,000 kcal/person/day**. Cold, hard work, pregnancy and recovery increase this to 2,500–3,500+.
- Store foods you actually eat, and rotate them. A stockpile you cannot cook is a museum.
- Include protein, fat, salt, and a source of fibre. Fat is calorie-dense and critical in cold weather.
- Do not restrict water to stretch food. Dehydration worsens everything.

## Shelf life (cool, dark, dry)
| Food | Typical storage |
|---|---|
| White rice, dry pasta | 5–10+ years (oxygen absorber, sealed) |
| Rolled oats | 2–5 years |
| Canned goods | 2–5 years (rotate) |
| Freeze-dried meals | 5–25 years |
| Honey | essentially indefinite |
| Salt, sugar | indefinite if dry |
| Cooking oil | 1–2 years; goes rancid |
| Nuts | 6–12 months; rancidity |
| Powdered milk | 2–5 years sealed |
| Pet food | rotate; check fat content |

## Preservation without refrigeration
- **Drying:** sun, air, or low heat. Meat and fish require careful temperature/humidity control and are high-risk; use proven recipes.
- **Salting/curing:** uses salt to reduce water activity. Follow tested ratios; do not improvise for meat.
- **Canning:** use a pressure canner for low-acid foods. Boiling-water canning is only for high-acid foods (fruit, pickles). Follow USDA times and altitude adjustments.
- **Fermentation:** vegetables in brine (2–5% salt) are relatively safe; protein fermentation is not beginner work.
- **Root cellar:** cool, dark, ventilated, rodent-proof. Store potatoes, carrots, onions, squash separately.
- **Freezing:** keep the freezer closed; a full freezer holds ~48 h, a half-full one ~24 h. Never refreeze thawed meat that stayed warm.

## Food safety
- Hot food >60 °C; cold food <4 °C.
- Discard perishables left >2 h at room temperature (1 h above 32 °C).
- Cook meat thoroughly; use a thermometer where possible.
- Wash hands, knives and boards. Separate raw meat from ready-to-eat food.
- Peel or cook produce from uncertain water. Do not drink unpasteurised milk.
- Botulism risk: low-acid canned goods with bulging lids, off smells, or cloudy brine are suspect. **Boil 10 minutes** before eating only if you must; when in doubt, discard.

## Foraging rules
1. **Never eat a plant or mushroom you cannot identify with certainty.**
2. Use at least two independent, local, expert sources — not an app, not a single photo.
3. Avoid plants near roads, industrial sites, sprayed fields, or polluted water.
4. Learn the dangerous look-alikes in your region first (e.g., poison hemlock vs wild carrot; death cap vs edible mushrooms).
5. Eat small amounts of a new food and wait; individual reactions vary.
6. Do not forage protected species or on private land without permission.
7. Wash and cook where possible. Animals and insects are also food, but require safe handling.

## Fishing, hunting and trapping
Follow local law and licences. Never set traps where people, pets or livestock can be injured. Field-dress game away from camp and water; cool meat quickly; cook thoroughly. In a survival emergency, prioritize fish, small game and invertebrates over large game that you cannot preserve.

## Gardening and production
- Start with crops that store well: potatoes, squash, beans, corn, cabbage, carrots, onions.
- Save open-pollinated seed; label and dry it.
- Water and soil are the limiting factors, not seed.
- A garden is a months-long project. Do not plan to feed a family from it in the first 30 days of a crisis.

## Rationing
- Eat regularly; do not "save" food until you are weak.
- Use a written inventory and a two-week menu.
- Keep a no-cook reserve for power outages.
- Include a manual can opener, fuel, and a way to boil water.
