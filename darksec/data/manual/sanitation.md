# Sanitation & Hygiene

> **In a disaster, disease spreads faster than supply lines recover.** Sanitation is life support: keep waste away from water, food, hands and shelter.

## Water and waste separation
- Latrine at least **30 m (100 ft)** from water sources, wells, springs and flood lines.
- Downhill and downwind of camp/shelter.
- 1 m deep, 30 cm wide; cover each deposit with soil/lime/ash. Keep a lid or tarp.
- Handwashing station beside the latrine: soap, water, and a foot-operated or gravity tap.
- Do not urinate in the same hole if it can be avoided; separate urine diversion reduces smell.
- If the water table is high or the area floods, use a bucket toilet with sealed bags and cover material; store waste above ground, away from living areas.

## Hand hygiene
- Wash with soap and water for 20+ seconds: before food, after toilet, after handling waste, before/after patient care.
- If water is scarce, use an alcohol-based hand rub (≥60%) when hands are not visibly dirty.
- Keep nails short; cover cuts. Gloves are not a substitute for handwashing.

## Human waste and greywater
- Bag and store waste securely if you cannot dig. Do not dump into streams, storm drains or streets.
- Greywater: strain solids, use for flushing or subsurface irrigation of non-food plants; never on edible crops.
- Disinfect buckets and tools with a 0.5% chlorine solution (1 part 5% bleach : 9 parts water), 10 minutes contact.

## Food hygiene
- Wash hands, utensils and surfaces. Separate raw meat.
- Cook thoroughly; keep hot >60 °C, cold <4 °C.
- Cover food; control flies, rodents and pets.
- Discard food left >2 h at room temperature (>1 h above 32 °C).
- Do not use contaminated water for washing dishes or food.

## Dead bodies
- Bodies generally do not transmit disease unless death was from a contagious disease or contamination is present.
- Use gloves; body bags or plastic sheeting; avoid contact with blood/fluids.
- Identify and document before burial/cremation if possible. Follow public-health instructions.
- Grieve and support each other; psychological first aid matters.

## Personal hygiene
- Wash face and hands daily; sponge bath with a cloth.
- Brush teeth with safe water; do not swallow.
- Keep feet dry; change socks; treat blisters.
- Launder clothing or sun-dry; lice and scabies spread in crowding.
- Menstrual products, diapers and infant supplies are critical; plan for them.

## Pests and vectors
- Eliminate standing water; cover water containers.
- Screen shelters; use nets; keep food sealed.
- Remove rubbish; bury or burn where legal and safe (never burn plastics near shelter).
- Use repellent and protective clothing; check for ticks daily.

## Chemical and radiological contamination
- Remove outer clothing and bag it; shower with soap and lukewarm water.
- Do not scrub hard or use hot water; flush eyes.
- Do not eat, drink or smoke until decontaminated.
- Keep clean and dirty zones separate; label waste.
