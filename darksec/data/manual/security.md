# Security & OPSEC

> **Avoid, detect, de-escalate, escape.** Force is a last resort and always a legal matter. The goal is to survive without becoming a threat to others.

## Baseline first
Know what normal looks like: vehicle traffic, foot traffic, sounds, animals, people's routines. Change is the signal. Record incidents in the log with time, location, description and action taken. Patterns emerge from records, not memory.

## OODA loop
**Observe → Orient → Decide → Act.** Under stress, slow down enough to orient. A fast wrong decision is worse than a deliberate right one. Reassess after acting.

## Home and camp security
- **Deter:** lighting, visibility, locks, clear boundaries, occupancy signs (not obvious fortification).
- **Detect:** door/window sensors, gravel, trip lines with bells (where safe/legal), neighbour watch, motion lights.
- **Delay:** sturdy doors, deadbolts, window film, reinforced hinges, safe room.
- **Respond:** a written plan, a rally point, a communication method, and a duress signal. Rehearse.
- **Avoid:** booby traps, illegal weapons, vigilantism, and confrontation over property. These escalate and are often criminal.
- Keep a fire extinguisher, first aid, water and a charged light in the safe area.
- Do not advertise supplies, generators, solar arrays or weapons.

## Patrolling and movement
- Plan route, checkpoints, rally points, and actions on contact.
- Move quietly, avoid skylines and open ground, use cover and concealment.
- Maintain 360° awareness; use the "one up, one back" formation with a leader.
- Establish signals (hand/whistle/radio) and a challenge/password.
- At night, preserve night vision; use red light sparingly.
- Avoid routines and predictable times. Vary routes.
- If followed: do not lead them home. Go to a public, lit, populated place; call for help.

## Checkpoints and unknown persons
- Stop at a distance, keep hands visible, stay behind cover, and communicate calmly.
- Ask open questions; watch hands and eyes; note descriptions and vehicles.
- Do not surrender your weapon/equipment without a legal basis. De-escalate and withdraw when possible.
- Never impersonate police or military. Never use false emergency calls.

## Communications security
- Assume phones, radios, vehicles and smart devices can be tracked.
- Keep sensitive plans in the encrypted vault; keep critical items on paper.
- Use pre-arranged check-in times and a missed-check-in plan.
- Strip metadata from photos; do not post locations, stockpiles or routes.
- Use code words for locations/actions if needed, but keep them simple and share only on a need-to-know basis.
- Turn off devices that beacon when silence matters.

## Crowds and civil unrest
- Avoid crowds and protests unless you must be there.
- Identify exits, cover, and a rendezvous. Keep your group together.
- Do not film or confront armed individuals. Leave.
- If trapped, move with the flow, keep low, and seek hard cover (not concealment).
- Document injuries/incidents after you are safe.

## Self-defence and the law
- Use only the minimum force necessary to stop an imminent threat to life, and only within the law. Retreat if you can.
- Understand your local rules on weapons, carry, storage and use of force. Ignorance is not a defence.
- Get training in de-escalation, verbal judo, and first aid. Avoid alcohol and aggression.
- Never use force to protect property when life is not at risk.

## Mental resilience and leadership
- Communicate clearly: who, what, when, where, why, how.
- Set routines: watch rotation, meals, hygiene, briefings, rest.
- Acknowledge fear and fatigue; give people useful tasks.
- Watch for scapegoating, rumour, and authoritarian drift. Consensus and transparency build trust.
- Plan for children, elderly, disabled and injured members. Their needs are not optional.

## The console
**Security** stores incidents, patients, notes (encrypted), OPORDs and communications plans. The **Map** holds waypoints, caches and rally points. The **Log** is your evidence trail. Use the **panic purge** only when you understand it: it deletes operator data and the vault but keeps the document library.

**Further reading:** *FM 3-39 Physical Security*, *FM 7-21.13 Soldier's Guide*, *Crisis Prevention Institute de-escalation*, and your local laws.
