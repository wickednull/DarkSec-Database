# Field Medicine

> **This is reference material, not a diagnosis or prescription.** Treat within your training and scope. Evacuate early when you can. The best medical skill is prevention.

## Primary assessment — MARCH
1. **Massive bleeding:** direct pressure, wound packing, tourniquet. Note tourniquet time.
2. **Airway:** open, clear, position. Head-tilt/chin-lift if no spinal concern; jaw thrust if trauma.
3. **Respiration:** look/listen/feel. Seal sucking chest wounds with a vented dressing. Treat tension pneumothorax only with training.
4. **Circulation:** pulse, skin colour/temperature, mental status. Treat shock: lay flat, keep warm, no food/drink.
5. **Head injury / hypothermia:** prevent secondary injury, protect the spine if mechanism suggests it, insulate.

## Vital signs
Record time, heart rate, respiratory rate, blood pressure, SpO₂, temperature, pain score and AVPU. Trends matter more than a single reading.
- **Shock index** = HR / systolic BP. >0.9 is concerning; >1.0 is critical.
- **qSOFA** ≥2 (RR ≥22, systolic ≤100, altered mental status) prompts urgent reassessment.
- **GCS** ≤8: airway is at risk; consider advanced airway.
- **NEWS2** is a track-and-trigger score; follow local protocol.

## Trauma
- **Bleeding:** pressure, packing, tourniquet. Do not remove a tourniquet once applied unless directed.
- **Chest trauma:** seal open wounds, monitor breathing, evacuate. Do not insert anything into a chest wound.
- **Abdominal trauma:** do not remove impaled objects; cover evisceration with a moist clean dressing; nothing by mouth.
- **Fractures:** immobilise, check pulse/sensation/movement before and after. Do not realign unless circulation is compromised and you are trained.
- **Burns:** cool 20 minutes, cover, no ice/butter. Parkland formula is a guide, not a prescription.
- **Head/spine:** keep still, monitor consciousness, avoid hypoxia.

## Medical emergencies
- **Anaphylaxis:** epinephrine IM outer thigh first (adult 0.3–0.5 mg; child 0.01 mg/kg, max 0.15 mg if <15 kg; junior 0.15 mg autoinjector for 15–30 kg). Use 1 mg/mL (1:1,000) IM, never dilute IV epinephrine. Repeat 5–15 min, call help.
- **Asthma:** sit upright, reliever inhaler, oxygen if available, evacuate if severe.
- **Cardiac arrest:** CPR 30:2, 100–120/min, AED. Compression-only if needed.
- **Stroke:** FAST, note onset, nothing by mouth, urgent evacuation.
- **Seizure:** protect, time, recovery position; >5 min or repeated = emergency.
- **Diabetes:** if conscious and low glucose, give sugar; if unconscious, nothing by mouth, call help. Do not give insulin unless prescribed and sure.
- **Sepsis:** fever, confusion, fast breathing, low BP; urgent antibiotics/evacuation. Do not delay.

## Environmental
- **Hypothermia:** gentle handling, dry, insulate, warm trunk, warm sweet drinks only if alert.
- **Heat stroke:** cool immediately, ice packs to neck/armpits/groin, call help.
- **Frostbite:** warm water 37–39 °C, do not rub, do not walk on thawed feet, protect.
- **Drowning:** remove from water, check breathing, CPR; consider hypothermia.
- **Lightning:** move to safety, triage; reverse triage may apply (treat apparently dead first).

## Medications
- Use only medications prescribed to the patient, in date, at the correct dose.
- Record every dose and time.
- Check allergies.
- Do not use leftover antibiotics empirically. Antibiotic resistance and wrong-drug harm are real.
- Keep a 30-day supply of critical medication, rotate it, and store it correctly.
- The console provides generic calculators (weight-based dose, IV rate, fluids). It does not validate a prescription.

## Infection control
- Gloves and eye protection for body fluids.
- Hand hygiene before/after every patient.
- Disinfect surfaces and instruments.
- Bag contaminated waste; keep it away from food and water.
- Needlestick: wash, report, seek urgent care.

## Triage in mass casualty
Use **START** for adults and **JumpSTART** for children. Sort, don't treat, on the first pass. Re-triage. The greatest good for the greatest number. Document a tag on each patient: name/callsign, triage colour, injuries, time, interventions.

## Evacuation decision
Evacuate early for: airway compromise, breathing difficulty, chest pain, severe bleeding not controlled, head injury with altered consciousness, abdominal pain, severe dehydration, suspected sepsis, fractures requiring surgery, burns >10%, pregnancy complications, or any patient you cannot monitor.
